My first SC: Chicago, science, and the people behind HPC

Sanjay Das · November 19, 2026 · 4 min read

My first SC brought me to Chicago’s McCormick Place for a week of agentic AI, trustworthy HPC, neuromorphic computing, and conversations with the people behind the systems.

A first SC, with plenty to share

There is something special about seeing research leave the page and become a conversation. At SC26, that meant presenting our work on trustworthy HPC analytics, joining the Early Career Program, and sharing SPIKE at the DOE booth. It also meant catching up with friends and meeting colleagues across laboratories, universities, and industry.

Sunday · Putting trustworthy HPC on the table

On November 15, I presented with Kartik Shinde at the AgenticAI4HPC’26 Workshop. Our paper, Recall Is Not Honesty, benchmarks multi-agent architectures for trustworthy HPC operational analytics. The central question is one we keep returning to: retrieving evidence is useful, but can we trust the conclusions drawn from it?

The workshop session ran from 9:00 AM to 12:30 PM in W183b. Our Trustworthy HPC research poster was also part of the week’s display in the Skyline Ballroom North Pre-Function, with display hours of 10:00 AM–5:00 PM.

Monday and Tuesday · Finding the community

Monday, November 16, was devoted to the Early Career Program. On Tuesday, the Mentor/Protégé Breakfast and the CRLC Labs/DOE ASCR Mixer offered more opportunities to connect across research groups and career stages.

Wednesday · Attention through spikes

On November 18, we shared SPIKE: Spiking Transformers for Neuromorphic AI at DOE Booth #4829, Station 2, from 4:00 to 5:00 PM. Led by ORNL’s Dr. Prasanna Date, the effort connects spiking attention, scalable simulation, and neuromorphic hardware.

For me, it was an opportunity to bring the idea of neuromorphic attention into a live demo setting: how might attention work when its building blocks are spiking neurons?

From HPC to aerospace

Another highlight was catching up with Darby Vicker and Jeff Triplett from NASA’s Flight Science Lab. Connecting with the NASA team alongside our AgenticAI4HPC collaborators from ORNL, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, and spinTwo brought another perspective to the week: the scientific and engineering problems that motivate the computing.

Conversations about the aerospace engineering research underway at NASA’s Flight Science Lab at Johnson Space Center were a reminder of how many communities meet at SC. See spinTwo’s update from the gathering ↗

Thank you—and a little Chicago

A huge thank you to my mentor, Dr. Tirthankar Ghosal, and my colleagues for their support. A shoutout, too, to Ethan Seefried, Vanessa Lama, Emily Herron, and everyone contributing sessions, demos, and posters throughout the week.

Beyond McCormick Place, these photographs preserve a little of the trip itself: the conference banners, familiar faces, and Chicago’s riverfront after dark. My first SC has been as much about those connections as the program.
